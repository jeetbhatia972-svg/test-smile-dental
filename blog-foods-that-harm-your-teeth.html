Foods that secretly harm your teeth, and what to eat instead

Sweets are the obvious culprit, but some everyday foods and habits are just as hard on teeth. Here are the ones to watch, and easy swaps that help.

The hidden troublemakers

  • Sticky foods such as dried fruit and chewy bars cling to teeth and keep feeding bacteria long after you finish eating.
  • Starchy snacks such as crackers, chips and white bread break down into sugars and get packed into the grooves of back teeth.
  • Acidic drinks including soda, sports drinks, energy drinks and fruit juice soften enamel, even the sugar-free versions.
  • All-day sipping of sweetened tea or coffee gives teeth no time to recover between acid attacks.
  • Sour sweets combine sugar and acid, a double hit for enamel.

Better choices

  • Water rinses away food and, where the water supply is fluoridated, strengthens enamel.
  • Cheese and plain yoghurt are rich in calcium and help neutralise acid.
  • Crunchy vegetables and apples stimulate saliva, your mouth's natural defence.
  • Nuts are low in sugar and make a tooth-friendly snack.
  • Sugar-free gum after meals boosts saliva flow.

A simple rule

If you are going to have something sweet, have it with a meal rather than grazing throughout the day. Your teeth handle a few short acid attacks far better than a constant stream.
